# DMA: one streaming engine, the scanout as a ring, and a bounded snoop (plan, 2026-10-24)

Tommy, 2026-10-23, on the scanout's glitches under load: "I would have had a ring of line buffers
(not 20, possibly just 2) and just continuously feed the ones that aren't being scanned. The only
reason to have more than 2 is if there's efficiency to be gained by burst filling them (= open
page). ... The same principles should apply to all DMA engines. Maybe we could even share the
implementation and save area?" Then: "I want to support much higher resolutions, possibly 4K",
"pixels remain hardwired at 16 bpp and 4K30 is already very good", and audio "only 44.1 kHz,
stereo @ 16-bit". From the memory system's plan (2026-10-21): "all DMA clients should have [at
least] two 64B buffers."

## Decisions (Tommy, 2026-10-23)

- **The scanout is a ring of line buffers, continuously fed**: the fetcher fills every buffer the
  scan is not reading, as soon as it frees, several reads in flight. More than two lines only where
  bursts buy efficiency (or latency tolerance).
- **One streaming engine for every DMA master**, the same principles everywhere; shared, if
  sharing saves area.
- **Pixels are RGB565, hardwired; the target is 4K30** (3840x2160, 297 MHz pixel clock), on the
  HDMI board that replaces TinyVGA.
- **Audio is 44.1 kHz, stereo, 16-bit**, nothing else.

## What is there today (8a69edc5)

Four DMA masters, all on `ui_clk`, **each with at most one transaction in flight**:

| master | moves | unit | LUTs / flops |
|---|---|---|---|
| `vga_scanout` | the framebuffer, read-only | one 64-byte burst, then the next (`F_AR -> F_R -> F_AR`) | 282 / 371, 2 RAMB36 |
| `virtio_net` | descriptors, frames, used ring | one 8-byte beat (`axi_single_beat_master`) | master 578 / 173 |
| `virtio_blk` | descriptors, sectors, status | one 8-byte beat; a sector is 64 serial beats | master 397 / 173 |
| `virtio_input` | descriptors, events | one 8-byte beat | master 178 / 172 |

- **The path**: three `axi_two_master_arbiter`s in the board top (net|blk, vga|kbd, then the two
  pairs), round robin, no priority; register slices; `ddr4_mem`'s AXI port, which has strict
  priority over the caches' line port; `ddr4_port_axi`, one read and one write at a time, each
  within one 64-byte line; the controller's queue. `arqos` is tied to 0 everywhere and nothing
  reads it.
- **Coherence, per transaction** (so per 8-byte beat for virtio): the request crosses
  `ddr_port_sync` as a toggle; `rv_soc_top` gives the D$'s outside port to the DMA or the I$'s
  clean in turns; the D$ takes it **only in a cycle the store port presents no store** (`ox_take`),
  behind at most two store-queue entries, and not while a CBO or NC store holds the store port;
  then the L2's snoop (served ahead of its requests, one lookup at a time); then the answer
  crosses back. A line dirty in the D$ costs a write-back into the L2, the L2's write-back to
  DRAM and the DMA's read, in series. DMA reads never allocate in or read from the L2.
- **The scanout fetches a line from its line's horizontal blank**: line L+1 is asked for at line
  L's blank, 20 bursts in series (640x480, a 1280-byte stride), and the fetch races the scan of the
  line it fills. A request that finds the fetcher busy is counted (`STATUS[31:16]`) and dropped:
  that line shows the buffer's old contents. **Measured on the 2 GiB board (10-23, e86e0bcf):
  55,094 late lines in 71 minutes** (a 16-bit counter that wraps: at least that many), nearly all
  under `cargo c` and the gate's Geekbench stress; idle, one in 30 s. Tommy saw the glitches.
- The scanout's bench (`src/tb_vga_scanout.v`) runs under `src/run-tb.sh`, which the gate did not
  run (only `core/run-*-tb.sh`); `src/run-tb.sh` exited 0 whatever failed.

## What the clients need

| client | rate | per 64-byte line | slack today |
|---|---|---|---|
| scanout, 640x480@60 | 37 MB/s | one every 1.6 us | one line time (31.8 us) for 20 serial bursts |
| scanout, **4K30** RGB565 | **0.52 GB/s** (7,680 B in a 14.8 us line) | one every 124 ns, 21 core cycles | none: needs 8-16 reads in flight at a 1-2 us loaded latency |
| virtio-net, 1 GbE | 125 MB/s each way | one every 0.5 us | one 8-byte beat at a time |
| audio, 44.1 kHz stereo 16-bit | 176.4 KB/s | one every 363 us | a 4 KiB ring is 23 ms |
| virtio-blk (SD over SPI, ~12 MHz) | ~1.5 MB/s | -- | -- |

At 4K30 the DRAM bandwidth is not the strain (0.52 of 10.67 GB/s raw); the per-line coherence is:
a D$ outside request and an L2 lookup every 21 core cycles, about a sixth of the L2's lookups.

## The design

### The engine
One block, `rv_dma`, with a channel per stream. A channel is a ring of 64-byte line slots in block
RAM and a descriptor: base, stride, count, direction. The engine keeps up to N line requests in
flight (a response names the slot it fills: matched by the tag the engine allocated), issues the next line as soon
as a slot is free, and raises a channel's **urgent** bit when its filled slots fall below a
threshold. One port into the memory system replaces the four masters and the arbiter tree. Each
device keeps its control plane (virtio's descriptor walking, the scanout's timing generator) and
moves its data through a channel, a whole line at a time instead of 8-byte beats -- so virtio's
snoops drop eightfold too. Whether one shared engine is smaller than four small ones is measured
(`make weigh` of today's masters: 1,435 LUTs together) before it is decided.

### The scanout as a client
A ring of line slots, filled from the frame's start continuously while the scan reads the oldest
full one; the timing generator only reads. An underrun -- the scan reaching a slot not yet full --
is counted, never silent. Two lines at 4K30 are 15 KiB, four RAMB36; the stride limit (4 KiB) and
the line buffer's 512 beats go. The pixel path takes two pixels a clock at 4K (148.5 MHz).

### The snoop's bound
The D$ takes an outside request only in a cycle with no store presented: under a store stream the
wait has no bound, and no buffering covers an unbounded wait. An outside request that has waited
N cycles holds the store port for one. Then, at the 4K rate, the snoop leaves the main lookup: the
third tag copy (the memory plan's "filter keeping the misses out of the lookup", until now waiting
for a measurement -- this is it) answers a clean snoop of a line the D$ does not hold without a
lookup cycle.

### Real time
The urgent bit travels with a channel's requests: `ddr4_port_axi` and the L2's snoop arbitration
serve an urgent request first. Only the scanout sets it; audio's ring covers 23 ms.

## Order

Each step is measured first, then gated as today (the lockstep battery, a build, the board).

1. **DONE 2026-10-24 (f92e3997, 6aaf00a3, bc785678).** The gate runs every `src/` bench. The D$'s
   outside request takes a slot whenever no store is taken and, refused four times with room,
   holds the store port's room off until it is in (the old rule fails the bound's check within
   800 cycles of the D$ bench; glibc's lockstep finishes 0.92% sooner). The scanout fills a ring of
   four line slots up to three lines ahead, four bursts in flight, starting in the vertical blank.
   On the 2 GiB board (gate PASS bc785678): **0 late lines** over the boot and the gate's 900 s of
   Geekbench, against 55,094 in 71 minutes before; the bench starved to 1% of its memory's
   acceptance counts 7, so the counter counts. Still to measure: `cargo c` beside it.
2. **The engine**, the scanout its first client; virtio-net and virtio-blk move their data through
   it a line at a time; `axi_single_beat_master` and the arbiter tree go.
3. **4K**: a 16 MiB framebuffer reservation in the device trees, the stride and line-buffer limits,
   two pixels a clock, the urgent class, the snoop filter if the measurement at 4K30 asks for it.
   The output is the HDMI board's.
4. **Audio**: a channel and the device (virtio-sound or a simple I2S ring), 44.1 kHz stereo 16-bit.
