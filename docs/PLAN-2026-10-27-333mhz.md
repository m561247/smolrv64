# PLAN 2026-10-27: a two-wide core at 333 MHz

Status: a study. Nothing here is built or committed as RTL. This document hands the idea to the
session that takes it on, together with every number measured so far and where its sources are.

Tommy, 2026-10-26: *"I wonder if we narrowed everything to half the lanes and gave every [stage]
twice the latency, we could clock at 333 MHz and thus meet the same performance in much less
area."* Then: *"The principle applies everywhere, including the frontend and the LSU."* The
relaxed scheduler should have 20 slots to be at parity with the core it replaces. Rarely used
operations (popcount and friends) can move to a slow pipe.

## 1. The idea

Two lanes at 333.33 MHz do as many operations per nanosecond as four at 166.67 MHz. If every loop
takes two cycles at 3 ns instead of one at 6 ns, every latency in nanoseconds stays the same. By
Little's law the work in flight stays the same as well: the same throughput times the same
latency. The rule for sizing is therefore:

- **A total stays and a per-lane size doubles.** The schedulers hold 2 x 20 entries (4 x 10 today).
  The ROB keeps its 256 entries (228 live), arranged as 128 rows of two. The LQ, SQ, decode queue,
  FP scheduler and landing buffers keep their totals.
- **Width-squared structures shrink.** These are rename's intra-group compares and map reads, the
  wake broadcast (lanes x entries x wake ports), the bypass network (lanes squared), the PRF's
  read-port copies (sharded by writer, its bits are read ports x registers), the ROB's completion
  ports and the commit count.
- **Arrays do not shrink.** The I$ and D$ (128 KiB each), the L2 (2 MiB), the BTB, TLBs and
  predictor tables keep their size; only their pipelines get deeper. The FPU is already one unit.
- **The clock crossings go.** The core runs on `ui_clk` itself: there is no 2:1 `ddr_port_sync`
  between core and memory, and the devices already run on `ui_clk`, so the MMIO bridge's and the
  keyboard FIFO's asynchronous crossings go too.

**The reference: today's core.** main c1f279ba (RTL 7f8f5a94): IW=4, 166.67 MHz, 150,870 LUTs,
CLB about 92% full. Geekbench 5 scores 20 single-core and 20 multi-core
(https://browser.geekbench.com/v5/cpu/24759238), at IPC 1.334. CoreMark is 5.50 per MHz and
Dhrystone 3.14 DMIPS per MHz.

**What has to be true.** IPC at 333 MHz must be at least half of today's IPC at 166.67 MHz on GB5,
and the design must close at 3.000 ns with its area comfortably below today's. Neither has been
measured for a whole core. Sections 4 and 5 hold everything measured so far.

The hardest case for the narrower core is code that already fills four lanes. `sha256sum` runs at
**IPC 2.95** on 7f8f5a94 (the board, 2026-10-27, Tommy's record). Two lanes at 333 MHz must sustain
1.48 of their 2.0 peak to match it, so it is the parity test that comes after GB5.

## 2. The alternatives, for the record

The other way up is to keep four lanes and raise the clock. `probe_clk` today is
BUFGCE_DIV(`ui_clk`)/2; only multiples of 3.000 ns time on that ladder. The DDR4 IP's unused
`ADDN_UI_CLKOUT1..4` outputs come from the PHY's own MMCM and could give a core clock in a fixed
ratio to `ui_clk`. Assuming a VCO of 1333.33 MHz (not yet confirmed), the candidates are:

| clock | ratio to `ui_clk` | endpoints that fail today's routed design at that period |
|---|---|---|
| 190.48 MHz (5.25 ns) | 4/7 | about 12,800 (0.75 ns short) |
| 200 MHz | none clean: asynchronous FIFOs (+4.5 cycles a DRAM access, measured) | 19,853 within 1.0 ns |
| 222.22 MHz (4.5 ns) | 2/3 | 38,366 within 1.5 ns |

These would be rational synchronous crossings, not asynchronous ones. The core alone out of
context at 5.0 ns misses by 1.947 ns on the -2 part, and by 1.962 ns on a -3 part (Tommy: "retry
with a -3 speed grade, just for fun"). The -3 part shortens the logic and lengthens the routes,
and failing endpoints fall only from 21,120 to 17,500: **a faster speed grade does not fix
depth.** With retiming switched on, 5.0 ns misses by 2.187 ns (module-default generics).

## 3. Today's timing, measured

## 4. The measured prices of a two-cycle loop at 166.67 MHz

These prices come from sim-only knobs run on the 77 GB5 SimPoints against main 4645a122 (results in
coffee's `~/exp-results/{alu2,alu2-sp,btb2,btb2-sp,m4645-sp}`). The knobs' RTL was never committed:

- `SMOLRV64_EXP_ALU2` delayed each lane's ALU wake by a cycle. The delayed wake (`we_d`) is gated
  by `~redirect`: an ungated wake reached the pending table after a redirect and tripped it.
- `SMOLRV64_EXP_BTB2` put one bubble after each predicted-taken pair.

| loop made two cycles | GB5 single-core cycles | elsewhere |
|---|---|---|
| ALU -> dependent ALU | **+4.11%**, integer subtests +4.6% to +9.4% | CoreMark +13.0%, Dhrystone +5.5%, pi +20%, 60 M boot -3.37% retires |
| prediction (BTB) | **+1.13%** (SQLite +7.0%, Text Rendering +5.5%) | Dhrystone +19.9%, CoreMark +6.2%, boot -1.82% |

These are cycle counts. They are what a four-wide design at 190 or 222 MHz pays when it pipelines
a loop, before its faster clock pays some of it back. At 333 MHz a two-cycle loop is the parity
case: 6 ns, as today. A **third** cycle at 333 MHz adds 3 ns where the knob added 6, so the knobs
are an upper bound on its cost. The fetch ring's loop has no price yet.

Two related measurements come from the integer-IPC study. Its knobs are on `wip/ipc`, kept in
`~/smolrv64-attic/2026-10-27/branches.bundle`; the PRF write's out-of-context sources are in
`~/smolrv64-attic/2026-10-27/prf2x/`:

- **A lane's single PRF write slot** carries both ALU results and landings, and 39% of integer loads
  wait in the landing buffer for it. A second write register per lane (`EXP_XLW`) is worth **-1.39%
  of GB5 cycles** (AES -6.96%); reserving the slot a cycle ahead (`EXP_LRSV`) loses instead (AES
  +0.28%). At 333 MHz with two lanes, each lane's slot carries twice the landings per cycle, so this
  ceiling matters more.
- **The PRF's write clocked on `ui_clk`, double-pumped:** one write slot per lane, written on both
  edges of `ui_clk`. Out of context, writes on the 2x clock meet timing with **+0.803 ns**, +291
  LUTs. It needs `set_max_delay 6.0` from the 2x clock to the core clock on the RAMs' read arcs,
  valid only because a register written mid-cycle is read through the bypass for that half cycle.
  At 333 MHz there is no 2x clock left, so this trick is not available there.

## 5. The 333 MHz calibration (out of context, 3.000 ns, `synth_design -retiming`)

Sources: `~/smolrv64-attic/2026-10-27/freq/lane333/`. `lane333.v` and `ooc.tcl` model one lane;
`btb333.v` and `btb-ooc.tcl` the predictor loop. Each script reads the repo's `core/smolrv64_exec.v`
and `src/` directly.

**One lane, 20 entries** (`lane333.v`, V2=0). The stages:

- S0 picks the lowest ready entry.
- S1 reads the picked entry's payload and broadcasts its destination as a wake: the lane's own, the
  other lane's, and the LD and FE streams'.
- S2 reads the operands from two 128-register LUTRAM shards, or from the result registers.
- S3 runs `smolrv64_exec`.

A dependent picked two cycles after its producer reads the producer's result register in S2: ALU to
ALU in 6 ns. The lane is 4,274 LUTs (684 of them LUTRAM) and 1,395 flops.

| stage | slack | levels | logic / route |
|---|---|---|---|
| S0 pick -> S1 entry register | +1.252 | 3 | 0.410 / 1.317 |
| S0 dispatch -> entry | +0.487 | 5 | 0.660 / 1.747 |
| **S1 wake -> ready (the scheduler loop)** | **+0.119** | 6 | 0.595 / 2.267 |
| S1 payload -> S2 | +0.483 | 1 | 0.151 / 2.347 |
| S2 operands -> S3 | -0.024 | 4 | 0.414 / 2.590 |
| **S3 execute (one-stage ALU)** | **-0.638** | 16 | 1.366 / 2.254 |

The scheduler loop fits at 3 ns with two cycles. The ALU does not fit in one stage.

**The ALU over two stages, left to retiming** (V2=1: a second register after the ALU, the result
register bypassed into S3a): **-1.157**, 19 levels in S3a and nothing in S3b. **Vivado did not
move the register into `smolrv64_exec`.** The split has to be written by hand, and section 6 is
the proposal.

**The BTB as a two-cycle loop** (`btb333.v`: 8 banks x 2 ways x 128 rows in block RAM, each bank's
output register as cycle A; the tag compare, direction, first-taken mark and the next pair's skewed
rows as cycle B, into the RAMs' address): **-0.293**, 10 levels (0.750 logic / 2.250 route), 829
LUTs and 16 RAMB36. Tommy: "BP will suffer." The options are a third cycle (priced in section 4 as
an upper bound), or a small level-0 BTB in flops that predicts back-to-back while the big one
overrides a cycle later.

**Other figures already on record:**

- The L2's URAM data array closes at 333 MHz with +0.1 ns out of context (`PLAN-2026-10-21-memory-system.md`).
- A block-RAM PRF with a register-read/bypass stage closed its read stage at 336 MHz out of context
  (2,152 LUTs + 32 RAMB36 against 5,032 LUTs of LUTRAM; memory note `uniform-lanes-end-state`).
- The whole design at 333 MHz (the 2026-09 two-wide core) missed by 2.492 ns, 39,389 failing endpoints
  (Spec §13). That was the old core with single-cycle loops, so it says nothing about this design.

## 6. The ALU over two stages: low half, then high half

Tommy's proposal (2026-10-27): *"a staggered ALU, low 32-bit followed by high 32-bit, but
unfortunately that barrel shifter needs a different treatment."* Stage 1 computes the low 32 bits
and its carry; stage 2 computes the high 32 bits from that carry. A dependent issued two cycles later
chains on the low half in stage 1 and the high half in stage 2, so back to back costs 6 ns, as today.

| class | stagger | note |
|---|---|---|
| add, sub, sh1add-sh3add, add.uw, and/or/xor, andn/orn/xnor, lui/auipc | yes | the carry is the only signal between halves |
| left shifts (sll, slli, slliw, sllw), sext.b/h, zext.h | yes | the low half of a left shift needs only the operand's low half |
| orc.b, bset/bclr/binv | yes | per byte; a bit index from the shift amount, which is in the low half |
| **right shifts, rotates and bext (srl, sra, ror, rol, bext)** | **no**: the barrel shifter | the low half of the result needs the operand's high half. Cheapest: the op waits for its source's full value and does the whole 64-bit shift in stage 1 (three LUT levels), then hands off like any other op. That costs +1 cycle only when its producer was a staggered op |
| W-forms (addw, subw, sllw, srlw, sraw) | stage 1 entire | the result is the low half sign-extended; srlw and sraw need only the low half too |
| slt/sltu, min/max, branch conditions, czero | stage 2 | the high half decides. Branch resolution moves a cycle later (+3 ns mispredict penalty); slt consumers wait a cycle |
| jalr's target | stage 2 | the link (pc + 4) is ready at once; the redirect needs the whole target |
| clz, ctz, cpop, rev8 | slow pipe | the count's or the result's low half depends on the operand's high half |
| address generation | yes | the low half carries the page offset, so the VIPT D$ starts its array read a cycle early. The VPN arrives a cycle later, when the dTLB and tag compare need it |

The first calibration is `lane333.v` with this hand-split ALU (a V3), plus a two-stage or one-stage
full-width right shifter. It answers whether stage 1 (a 32-bit adder, the shifter, the operand
mux) and stage 2 each fit in 3 ns with the bypasses.

## 7. The rest of the machine, by segment (to design)

- **Frontend.** One 16-byte pair per two cycles keeps today's fetch rate (`btb333.v` models this). The
  fetch ring's next-head loop (25 levels at 5 ns) has not been calibrated at 333 MHz and is the most
  likely place for a design change. The I$'s virtual hit (VHPR) becomes a pipeline of array, tag
  and select.
- **Decode, queue, rename.** Two-wide: the intra-group compare is 2 squared, and the map is read 4
  times a cycle (8 at IW=4). Rename's low-water marks scale with IW.
- **Schedulers.** Two lanes of 20 entries, oldest-first pick as today (`OLDEST`), with the wake
  ports of the calibration. The FP scheduler keeps its total.
- **PRF.** S2 operands are at -0.024 with LUTRAM. A block-RAM PRF with a read stage (measured at
  336 MHz, section 5) is the alternative.
- **Multiply and divide.** The multiplier gets twice the stages. The divider stays C910's (Tommy
  2026-10-25) and needs a multicycle constraint or the half-rate treatment.
- **LSU and D$.** The 128 KiB VIPT D$ becomes a pipeline (arrays, tag and dTLB compare, way select,
  align); the work is the deeper pipe's store hazards. Load-to-use stays the same in ns: today 3
  cycles to an address (18 ns) means 6 at 333. Going straight to the L2 for loads is out: an L2 hit
  is 21 cycles load-to-load today (Tommy: "never mind").
- **FPU.** The cvfpu FMA runs 39 levels and needs about twice its stages. FP structures are
  area-limited and give way to integer (never trade integer for FP).
- **ROB and commit.** 128 rows of two, commit two a cycle. Today's completion-port count, 2 x NL + 5,
  gives 9 at two lanes.
- **SoC.** The core on `ui_clk`; `ddr_port_sync` and the MMIO bridge's asynchronous FIFOs go away.

## 8. Order of work for the new session

1. **Calibrate before writing core RTL.** Run out of context at 3.000 ns with `-retiming`:
   - the hand-split ALU in `lane333.v` (section 6);
   - the fetch ring's next head;
   - the BTB with a third cycle or with a level-0 BTB;
   - rename at two-wide;
   - the LQ/SQ address compares;
   - the D$'s worst stage.

   Each is a small module and a few minutes of Vivado.
2. **Estimate IPC before building.** Today's RTL can model the 333 MHz machine at IW=2
   (`SMOLRV64_IW=2` still builds), with:
   - the schedulers at 20;
   - ALU and prediction loops at two cycles (re-create `EXP_ALU2` and `EXP_BTB2` from section 4);
   - every pipeline latency doubled.

   Its GB5 SimPoint cycles at 3 ns against today's at 6 ns decide whether ops per ns hold.
3. **Estimate the area** with `make ooc HIER=1` of the IW=2 core at those sizes, against 150,870
   LUTs today.
4. **Take the go or no-go to Tommy** with those three answers.
