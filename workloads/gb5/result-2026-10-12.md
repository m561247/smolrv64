# SmolRV64 -- Geekbench 5.4.1 (RISC-V preview), 2026-10-12, single-core

Board build of bbd27c9a (main): IW=3; on top of 2026-10-06's 1d84a62f, fetch decoupled from
dispatch (twice the dispatch width into a 16-deep queue, start bits predecoded in the ring, the
predictor with path history at the fetch stream, trained only by non-speculative control
transfers), M filling a load or store every cycle through a two-deep D$ read door, loads and
stores out of program order with memory-dependence speculation (a 16-entry load queue, an
8-entry store queue), a radix-4 divider, every "at the ROB head" decision a flop, and the 4 GiB
memory controller (one bitstream for both DDR4 parts, at the slower of each part's timings).
166.67 MHz, Explore placement (WNS +0.006), Ubuntu over NFS on the 2 GiB board. Multi-core 14
(one core). Three runs of this bitstream counted different sets of events: the second took 0.06% more
cycles than the first, and the third, which printed its counts every second, 0.18% more. The
scores are the second's. The tag gb5/bbd27c9a quotes an earlier run
(24713112) that shared the PMU with a per-second counter logger -- 26% longer, its counts perf's
scaled estimates, its scores depressed.

## What the back-end waits for (the second run)

The same work to within 0.0007% of the instructions took 0.060% more cycles and 0.068% more wall
time than the first run: that is the spread between two runs. Its counters swap the front-end's depth, the redirect causes and the
caches for the dispatch-stall causes (`ST_*`, overlapping wait-cycle counts, not a partition),
as shares of the cycles:

| | share |
|---|---|
| the queue head's scheduler has no credit (`ST_IQ`) | 13.96% |
| no store-queue credit (`ST_SQ`) | 2.62% |
| no load-queue credit (`ST_LQ`) | 1.23% |
| a serializing op draining or in flight (`ST_SRZ`) | 0.23% |
| a rename shard below `LOWAT` (`ST_RN`) | 0.0007% |
| M stalled on the CVFPU, or no issue while an entry waits for an FP result (`ST_FPU`) | 41.48% |

A scheduler, not the load or store queue, is 78% of the dispatch waits that the "no scheduler,
load or store queue room" bucket (23.85% here) is made of; rename never stops dispatch, and
serialisation hardly does, so the back-end's "rest" (21.56%) is M held on a non-memory op and
the wait for FP results.

Dispatching, bad speculation, front-end and back-end partition the cycles. Memory, ROB, queues
and rest partition the back-end. Slots is the share of the three dispatch slots filled
(`DPATCH` over three per cycle). The queues bucket also holds the cycle after each wait, when the
queue head's group refills the IR: the classifier charges it to the wait's cause, so the bucket
exceeds `ST_IQ` + `ST_SQ` + `ST_LQ`, which count only the waits. `ST_IQ` is the queue head's own
scheduler: lane A's for an integer op (slot k is lane k), the FP/MD/SYS scheduler's for an op of
that class.

- **Integer:** the section dispatches in 48% of its cycles and fills 43% of the slots.
  - Its largest wait is the queue head's credit, at 24%: its scheduler 9.8%, the store queue
    6.9% (13-14% in Navigation, HTML5 and SQLite), the load queue 1.4%.
  - The front-end holds 11% (Clang 36%, SQLite 25%, HTML5 18%).
- **Floating point:** the section dispatches in 36% of its cycles; `ST_FPU` reads 54%.
  Gaussian Blur and Structure from Motion take 1,733 s of the single-core pass's 3,754. They
  dispatch in 36-39% of their cycles, at `ST_FPU` 48-62% and `ST_IQ` 18.5%.
- **ROB room:** Machine Learning waits for it in 56% of its cycles, Face Detection in 28%.
- **Crypto:** AES-XTS dispatches in 55% of its cycles and fills 55% of the slots, the most of
  any subtest.

The multi-core pass (one thread on the one core) repeats the single-core figures within about a
point. The exceptions are Navigation, Text Rendering, Ray Tracing and Speech Recognition, which
run for different lengths in that pass (Ray Tracing 223 s against 49 s, at IPC 0.78).
