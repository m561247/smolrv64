instructions: 5.97 D$ line fills (5.83), 0.95 I$ misses (0.98), 2.63 conditional-branch and 0.47

what it held moved to the next structures in line: the schedulers and the load and store queues
(+145 G) and the rest of the back-end (+137 G). Net, 73.6 G cycles left the run. The same core's
checkpoint slices (50 M instructions each) split that rest: in the long FP subtests it is mostly
an op waiting on an FP result (`be:dep-fp`: Gaussian Blur 14.9%, Camera 13.2%, Structure from
Motion 7.0% of the slice's cycles) and rename below its low-water mark (Structure from Motion 9.4%,
Camera 7.4%, Gaussian Blur 2.5%); HTML5's slice has neither (both 0.0%) and waits on its schedulers
instead (`be:iq-full` 9.5%).

The BTB took 14.6 G cycles out of the front of the machine -- the front-end -7.1 G (its iMMU-walk
and no-fetch-bytes part -3.8 G), bad speculation -3.1 G, dispatching -4.4 G -- and the back-end
gave 18.9 G back, most of it in the memory bucket and in full queues: load latency.
The board's memory set (`tools/board/membench`, run on both bitstreams on fresh boots; `chase2`
counts its own user-mode cycles) measures the two latencies the series moved. A load that misses
the D$ in a page the dTLB's first level holds reaches its dependent load in 46.6 cycles, against
42.1 on 258ef1fd: the clock-crossing FIFOs' synchronizer stages, +10.7% on every DRAM access, which
no checkpoint slice shows because the lockstep model always crossed through them. A load whose
page only the dTLB's second level holds takes 5 to 9 cycles more, where 258ef1fd's 2048-entry first
level held it. A D$ hit is 5.7 cycles on both.
