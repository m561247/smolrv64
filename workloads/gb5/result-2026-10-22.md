# SmolRV64 -- Geekbench 5.4.1 (RISC-V preview), 2026-10-22, our DDR4 controller and the synchronous crossing

Board build of the memory path that replaces the DDR4 IP's controller: our controller (`src/ddr4_ctl.v`)
on the IP's PHY-only port, its bank hashing the row's low bits, and the core's memory port crossing into
ui_clk as registers (`src/ddr_port_sync.v`) instead of asynchronous FIFOs: the RTL of 8f970165, run from
the build of its sources made before the commit (the board printed `rtl=870f70d6+`); the gate stamped
that same build as 8f970165 and passed. 166.67 MHz, WNS 0.000 ns, gate PASS on the 2 GiB board; Ubuntu over NFS,
run alone under `perf stat` (the `td` set, every counter at 100%); no faults. **Single-core 18,
multi-core 18 (one core): the first 18.**

Every timed subtest is faster, from +1.6% (Rigid Body Physics) to +10.0% (Clang). Clang, N-Body,
Navigation and Speech Recognition move most: the line fills they wait on are 9 cycles shorter. Clang's
gain is two thirds instruction-side -- 8.1 I$ misses per 1000 instructions on 414eda07, against 0.4 to 1.3
for most subtests -- a unified L2's first customer.

Two runs led here on the same day: c8c7f410 (the controller behind the FIFOs,
https://browser.geekbench.com/v5/cpu/24744125, 1.0380 T cycles, -1.51%) and 8f3f4650 (with the bank
hash, https://browser.geekbench.com/v5/cpu/24744890, 1.0370 T cycles, -1.61%); both scored 17.
