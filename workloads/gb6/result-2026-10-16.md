**The spread is the instruction stream's, not the core's.** A score is the baseline's work per
second, so score / IPC is, up to one constant, the baseline's work done per instruction executed.
From the best subtest to the worst the score spans 48x, the IPC 2.5x and the work per instruction
93x. The five lowest scores (Object Detection 1, Background Blur 2, Photo Library 3, Photo Filter
4, Structure from Motion 6) run at IPC 0.76-1.28, three of them above the median subtest's 1.00,
and execute 1.9x (Structure from Motion) to 12.5x (Object Detection) the geometric mean's
instructions per unit of work. These are the image and machine-learning
kernels that vector units carry on the baseline machine. The RISC-V preview ships two binaries,
`geekbench_rv64gcv` (879,718 vector instructions in its text) and `geekbench_riscv64` (2,498), and
a core without V runs the second. The core's own weak points are the low-IPC rows. Object Remover
(0.53) and Background Blur (0.76) spend 60-69% of their cycles in "the rest", and Background Blur
fills 15.9 D$ lines per 1,000 instructions. Navigation (0.62) waits on memory (26.7%) with 18.7
conditional mispredicts and 13.6 D$ fills per 1,000 instructions. Clang (0.68) is front-end bound
(38.5%, 13.9 I$ misses per 1,000).
